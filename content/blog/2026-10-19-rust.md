**1. Sockets and async (start here)**
- `std::net` (`TcpListener`, `TcpStream`) for blocking basics.
- **Tokio** for real work. Its official tutorial (tokio.rs/tokio/tutorial) builds a mini-Redis server and client, which covers framing, shared state, and graceful shutdown.
- `tokio-util` codecs (`Framed`, `LengthDelimitedCodec`) for protocol framing, and the `bytes` crate for buffers.
- **socket2** when you need options `std` doesn't expose (`SO_REUSEPORT`, keepalive tuning, buffer sizes, `TCP_NODELAY` variants). **mio** if you want the epoll layer under Tokio.

**2. Packets and raw access**
- `pnet` for raw sockets and packet parsing and building.
- `etherparse` for fast, zero-copy header parsing.
- The `pcap` crate (libpcap bindings) or `pcap-file` for capture and replay.
- `tun-tap` or the `tun` crate for virtual interfaces. On Linux, use these with `ip tuntap` for safe experiments.

**3. Implementing TCP/IP yourself**
- [**smoltcp**](https://github.com/smoltcp-rs/smoltcp) is a standalone, `no_std`-friendly TCP/IP stack and the best code to read. It's small enough to follow.
- Jon Gjengset's ["Implementing TCP in Rust" ](https://youtu.be/bzja9fQWzdA?si=b4bRzVf1Ri24WE9l)live-stream series on YouTube builds TCP over a TUN device from scratch. It's long but excellent.
- *Rust in Action* (McNamara) has a networking chapter that builds a stack over a TAP device.

**4. Protocol theory (language-agnostic)**
- *TCP/IP Illustrated, Vol. 1* (Stevens/Fall) is still the reference for how TCP actually behaves.
- RFCs 9293 (TCP), 791 (IP), and 793 for history.
- Beej's Guide to Network Programming. It's written for C, but the socket concepts map directly.

**Practical tips**
- Start with an echo server, then a chat server, then a protocol with real framing.
- Test with `ncat`, `tcpdump`, and Wireshark so you see what's on the wire. `tc netem` simulates loss and delay.
- Newer stacks like [QUIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QUIC) ([`quinn`](https://quinn-rs.github.io/quinn/)) are worth a look once TCP makes sense.
